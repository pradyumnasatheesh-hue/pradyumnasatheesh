Case study. Hyderabad EXO and the ONE card, a systems thinking project

A city of 11 million, one guide to what makes it itself, and one card to get you there.

    How the rebuild keeps thinking in systems
    Most of what I added works on Meadows' point 6, information flows: putting information where it was missing.
    • Who vetted a place, and why it ranks where it does, shown on the screen.
    • The fare for every leg, shown inside the plan.
    • Money that has not reached the card yet, shown as "Waiting to load".

    The card as a product

    A card has no screen. So I designed everything around it.

    Rs 500 loadedBalance Rs 920

    Modern card rules I held to
    • Numberless face. The full number is never printed or shown.
    • Portrait, because that is how a card is held at a gate.
    • One colour per tier, taken from the app's five materials.
    • A notch at the reader end that fingers can find.
    Three forms, one account
    Physical cardBalance on the chip. Works with no signal.
    In-app cardThe Pass tab is the wallet: load, freeze, alerts, history.
    Scan passA refreshing code for visitors and forgotten cards.
    The constraint that shaped it most
    On India's common mobility cards, money added online reaches the chip only at the next tap. Most apps hide that. I made it a screen.

    So Pass shows two balances, "On the card" and "Waiting to load", and the gate confirms the moment it lands.

    Fixed by how the standard works
    • A RuPay prepaid card with a stored balance on the chip.
    • That balance is what a gate reads, so taps work offline.
    • Apple Wallet is not an option for this card today.
    Assumed, to be validated
    • Gates and buses with code scanners.
    • Reading the card over NFC on Android.
    • Tier names, pass lengths and every price.
    What I took away

    A product is the screens plus everything that happens when there is no screen.

    The app was the easy half. The card made me design a counter, a gate, a sound and a refund. Next I would test whether riders understand "Waiting to load" on first read.
